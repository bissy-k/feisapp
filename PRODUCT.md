# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Irish dance students and competitive dancers preparing for feiseanna, practicing at home or in a studio. Often practicing solo, mid-movement, or with limited attention to spare for the screen — the interface is a practice companion, not the focus of attention. The job to be done: play a tempo-accurate reference track or metronome, adjust it live (speed, which instrumental layers are audible), and stay in rhythm without breaking practice flow to fight the UI.

## Product Purpose

A mobile-first practice tool that gives dancers precise, adjustable playback (tempo, stems, metronome) for rehearsing steps to music. It exists because generic music players don't support the tempo/step-pattern control a dancer actually needs mid-practice. Success looks like a dancer being able to glance, tap, and get back to dancing — every control has to survive being used one-handed, mid-movement, without sustained visual attention.

## Brand Personality

Warm and encouraging, with a playful, energetic edge. Feels like a supportive practice companion, not a clinical tool or a professional studio console. Soft, rounded, approachable — confidence without severity.

## Anti-references

No strong constraint from the user on visual sources to avoid, beyond one implicit one: this specific feature (a stems mixer) tempts a dense, dark, technical DAW/mixing-console look. Resist that pull — even though the underlying concept is a mixer, it must stay legible and soft within this app's existing warm/cream language, not read as a professional audio-engineering tool bolted onto a dance app.

## Design Principles

- Reachable mid-motion: every interactive control must work for a distracted, one-handed, low-attention tap or drag — err toward larger touch targets and forgiving hit areas over visual density.
- Warm over technical: even functionally precise features (mixer, metronome, tempo dial) should read as approachable practice tools, not professional audio equipment.
- Momentum over completeness: a dancer should never have to stop and study the screen to use it — glanceable state, obvious affordances, minimal reading required mid-session.
- One accent, used deliberately: the coral accent (#E56D56) marks the single primary action or active state per view; it does not multiply across simultaneous elements.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific accessibility requirements flagged beyond general good practice. Favor generous touch/drag targets given the "used mid-movement, one-handed" usage context noted above.
